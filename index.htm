Building a New Home in Ellenbrook? 6 Fencing Decisions Worth Making Before Moving In

Building a new home is an exciting milestone, but there are several outdoor details to organise before moving in. Fencing is one of them. The right fence can improve privacy, define your boundaries and provide security while complementing your home's design. If you are planning fencing Ellenbrook homeowners need for a new property, these six decisions will help you prepare.

Home Improvement Ellenbrook, Western Australia Fencing Planning Guide

1. Decide What You Want Your Fence to Achieve

Start by identifying the main purpose of your fence. Different properties have different requirements, and understanding your priorities makes choosing a suitable design easier.

  • Privacy: Create a more enclosed backyard for family activities and outdoor relaxation.
  • Security: Establish a defined boundary and help manage access to your property.
  • Appearance: Choose a design that complements your home's exterior.
  • Practicality: Consider pets, children, garden layouts and everyday access.

You may need different fencing styles for the front, sides and rear of your property. Planning these areas separately can help you make better decisions.

2. Choose the Right Fencing Material

Your fencing material affects the overall appearance, maintenance requirements and suitability of the installation. Consider how the material will work with your home's architecture and outdoor environment.

Colorbond Fencing

Colorbond fencing is worth considering if you want a solid boundary with privacy and a consistent appearance. Its range of colours can help coordinate fencing with other exterior features.

Garrison and Security Fencing

Garrison fencing offers an open vertical-bar design that can suit properties where visibility and boundary security are priorities. It may be particularly useful for selected front boundaries and access areas.

Pool Fencing

If your new home includes a swimming pool, consider pool fencing early in the planning process. The design and installation must comply with applicable Western Australian pool safety requirements.

3. Plan Gates and Everyday Access

A fence should make everyday movement around your property convenient. Think about where people, vehicles and equipment will need to enter and exit.

Pedestrian Gates

Consider convenient access to gardens, side passages and backyard areas.

Driveway Gates

Plan vehicle entry, available clearance and practical opening space.

Discuss gate positions and opening directions with your fencing contractor before finalising the fence layout.

4. Confirm Boundaries and Ground Conditions

Before installation, confirm where your property boundaries are and review the condition of the proposed fence line. New properties may still have unfinished landscaping, uneven ground or construction materials around the yard.

Check for sloping sections, retaining walls, garden beds and underground services. If the boundary is uncertain, seek appropriate surveying advice rather than relying on existing temporary markers.

Important: If the fence is shared with a neighbouring property, discuss the proposed work and responsibilities before installation. Also check relevant local requirements where applicable.

5. Set Your Fencing Budget Before Moving In

Fencing costs depend on several factors, including material selection, total fence length, height, gates and site preparation. Establishing a realistic budget early can help you prioritise essential work alongside other moving-in expenses.

When comparing written quotations, check that they include:

  • Material type and specifications.
  • Fence measurements and installation scope.
  • Labour and site preparation charges.
  • Gate installation and additional components.
  • Removal of existing materials, if required.
  • Expected timeframe and warranty information.

Compare quotations based on the same specifications instead of choosing solely by the lowest price.

6. Choose a Fencing Contractor Early

Leaving fencing until the last minute can make coordinating your move-in preparations more difficult. Contact a fencing contractor early enough to discuss your plans, arrange a site assessment and understand the expected installation schedule.

Review previous project examples, relevant experience, customer feedback and the clarity of the quotation. A contractor who communicates clearly can help you understand material choices and installation requirements before work begins.

Pre-Move-In Fencing Checklist

Before finalising your new home's outdoor space, make sure you have considered these essentials:

✓ Fencing purpose identified
✓ Suitable materials selected
✓ Gates and access planned
✓ Property boundaries confirmed
✓ Installation budget prepared
✓ Contractor and timeline arranged
